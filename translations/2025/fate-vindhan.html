தலையெழுத்து | Fate

Vindhan

In each of our lives we make many friends; we meet them on our journeys in trains and buses, or in a theatre while watching a movie or maybe a play. These friendships are often short-lived. I too made some new friends at the beach. They were Senkannan and Karunkannan. But it was not short- lived; I would see them every time I visit the beach. They trusted me a lot and I did not quite understand why. Maybe it was because of the Khadi that I wore? Maybe. People tend to think that all those who wear Khadi are Gandhi! When I came to the beach that day, Senkannan and Karunkannan arrived on the shore in their boat. “How was the catch today, Senkanna?” I asked. “What catch, these ruinous winds will be our end! If you as much as blink an eye it will also take us along!” said Senkannan. “I wish it would, it would be a better end than wishing to die every day!” said Karunkannan. I laughed. “Saami* what’s funny?” “I hope to live atleast for another decade. Looks like you would be happy to see us gone sooner.” “We desire that which gives us happiness don’t we saami?” “Don’t you desire to be alive then?” “For that we need to have something like a life.” “Who stopped you from having a life? If you work hard you will prosper in life!” He laughed. “What is so funny?” I asked him. “It is funny that you think working hard can make us prosper.” “Why not?” “Why will it Saami? Our fates have been sealed long back.” “What fate?” “We work hard so that Naina Mohammed will prosper!” “Who is that? Is that the guy who got the house at Irulappa street for 8000 rupees at the auction?” “Yeah him. You would have seen him on a horse wagon.” “Why does he prosper because of you?” “You know why Saami! We could even sell each of these fishes for 5 rupees. But the kind soul that he is, he gives us only three rupees for it.” “Why do you sell to him then? Why don’t you sell to someone that would give you the right price?” “How can we Saami? To rework our huts we got 50 rupees each from him last month. As per this deal they say he would give Rs. 3 for a basket of fishes, and he will take 1 rupee a day as repayment for loan.” “If you had saved money for reworking your huts you wouldn’t have taken a loan from this Naina Mohammed and you wouldn’t have had to incur a two rupee loss for your fishes each day.” “Of course Saami, ifs and buts could make me not have any loss in life!” “Exactly. If there was no loan there would be no loss.” “Ifs! You see, that kind soul would come and give two rupees after deducting the one rupee for loan. Do you think we would take it back home? That won’t happen. Our bodies tired from all the hard work from morning doesn’t move an inch without beer in the belly!” “Even cows and crows work hard. Do they all have beer before going to sleep?” “We have that one right that they don’t have. Should that too be taken away from us?” “Don’t you want your troubles to go away?” “Saami, do you think if beers are taken away from us we won’t have any troubles. It is our troubles that should be taken away from us!” “If you keep drinking like this who will feed your family?” “Saami, they too will do coolie somewhere and take care of themselves.” “So you mean all your responsibility ends when you become fathers of one or two!” “Hmm. Saami, don’t make us feel horrible. We can’t help it.” “Really! First stop with all this ‘Saami’ pleasing and think that you are also equally respectable Saami yourselves” “Who knew that we can think ourselves into becoming a Saami!” “You should know better about this Naina Mohammed. He sells off those fishes that he gets from you for a lot more and hoards wealth. He has a posh home, gourmet food to feast on, has his horse wagon for a comfortable ride, he lives the life. Would he even know what suffering is? No way, no way at all. You work hard and he reaps the rewards. He was just buying fishes from you; now he has found the price to buy you! If only you sell your fishes to someone else you could also have prospered like him” “We can dream about these Saami!” “Efforts will make dreams come true!” “God’s fate is set for eternity and our fate is to suffer; Naina Mohammed’s fate is to prosper!” he said. Anger boiled inside me. ‘They can’t spell fate to save their lives, yet that is what seems to make their lives. When will my country wake up!’ I lamented to myself, got up and left. I made sure I never saw them again. *"சாமி" ("Saami") means "God," "lord," or "master," derived from the Sanskrit "swami." It serves as a respectful or affectionate term to address elders, superiors, spiritual figures, or even close friends in informal contexts.
