Marriage of Maria Braun

With the second world war nearing the end, and the Nazis after years of committing genocide are on the verge of defeat, Maria Braun marries her love Hermann. A new Germany is right on the horizon. From the rubble of a defeated country, she leads her family to an economic miracle. Hermann, being an SS army soldier, has to leave to Soviet Russia even before their marriage is consummated. The Nazis were soundly defeated by the Red army and received the final blows of their fascist campaign. Maria’s hunger for economic upliftment, arises from the broken German man. The lack and absence of them. ​Germany is now being governed by the allied forces and Maria aches for the return, and searches for her missing husband. Her friend, Betti is also in a similar situation. During this time, transactions become the norm even inside families. Supplies are hard to come by and each thing is evaluated and exchanged for another. Fed up with the unfruitful search for Hermann, she gives up on him and gets a job as a barmaid. Meanwhile, Betti’s husband, Willie, returns and announces that Hermann is dead. Heartbroken at this, Maria begins to move on from her past. She gets into a relationship with Bill, an African-American, whom she had met in the bar. Maria is fond of him and sleeps with him. He even helps her learn English. He offers a much-needed solace, to her and the family. ​She seems to be getting along well in her new relationship, until one fine day, when Hermann suddenly shows up. He has come back, wearied by war, only to witness his wife sharing the bed with Bill. As drama ensues and Hermann is overpowered by Bill, Maria sides with her husband to strike down Bill. Bill dies in the quarrel. ​In the trial that follows, Maria is disappointed that the investigators aren’t sensitive enough to understand the difference between her fondness for Bill and her love for Hermann. Hermann interrupts the proceedings and confesses to the crime. He is sent to prison. But Maria looks unfettered and in fact even strangely hopeful, as she promises her husband – upon his release from the prison – a prosperous life. Her fondness for Bill ends with an abortion. Clutching on to the prospect of building a life that her husband would be proud of, she starts out on a journey. ​That’s when she meets Oswald, a French business magnate, on the train. Flanking her English – that she had learnt from Bill – and her charming persona, she instantly creates an impression on him. Despite the skepticism of his accounts-keeper Senkenberg, Oswald offers her a job. And when Maria plays a crucial role in brokering a deal with an American company, Oswald falls completely for her. Hermann, Bill, Oswald, Willie – are not just the men that Maria comes across in her life but are representatives of various stakeholders who were a part of, and were affected by the Germany’s post-war economic miracle. ​Maria informs about all of her exigencies to her husband, even about her relationship with Oswald. She holds onto the marriage as the singular ideal and salvation. And everything and everyone revolve around that. The marriage, in a way, is symbolizing Germany’s economic revival. Maria in wanting to achieve the reunion with her husband manipulates her circumstances and people to achieve power and wealth. ​Though Maria warns Oswald that she can never marry him, she doesn’t even bother to tell him about her marriage or husband. Oswald eventually finds out about Hermann. In a desperation to keep Maria for herself, he writes away half of his assets to her. He then promises the remaining half to Hermann, by making a secret deal with him - asking him to leave Maria and emigrate to Canada, upon his release. When Senkenberg confronts Oswald over this being an act of madness, he says, “I live in a country of that name”. ​Meanwhile, Maria has supported both Willie and Betti to get ahead in life. She helps Betti in setting up her own business and Willie is with her when she is being the ‘Mata Hari of the economic miracle; “a tool of capitalism by day; an agent of working classes by the night” She tries to pull some strings to get her husband out of jail, but doesn’t succeed. Betti is the sole consolation for Maria in her difficult moments. We see a different Maria with her. A more tender and honest Maria. She cries her troubles out on the shoulders of Betti, speaks frankly about her situation with Oswald, and tells her what she really feels about people and life. Willie is another such person, to whom Maria is herself. Wealth has now well and truly arrived in Maria’s life. They even have a birthday party for her mother. After successively navigating the difficult post-war years they now have copious amounts of food and drink, and other things take precedence in their lives. Their concerns are no more about survival. Willie and Betti spiral into an unhappy marriage. Willie feels Betti is not an ideal woman like Maria, and Betti thinks she has become too fat. Oswald is aware of Maria’s husband, but chooses not to talk it out with Maria. He just waits for the love and hand of Maria with expecting eyes. Grandpa Berger, who is from the past century, and “who never wakes up and never sins”, sits immobile, inexpressive and without any agency. Only Maria’s mother seems to have genuine happiness on her face, with new found love. Still, Maria doesn’t seem to enjoy it all very much and in a striking scene, she dances her way through the hands of all her family members, friends and lover. A microcosm of the German revival. She has held the family (and country presumably) together and uplifted it. But without Hermann she never finds fulfillment. So, she goes to prison and offers all of her wealth to him. And when he shuts her out, her life’s meaning is in question: “I have led this life for you, for us!” she is confused. It seems that, after the war, women did the hard work and men had their plans. From then on, Maria gets more bitter with life. She becomes rich enough to even buy a new bungalow. Only Hermann has to come back to make her life complete. But when he gets released, as per the deal with Oswald he leaves to Canada informing Maria through a letter, “I’m going to become a better man” and that she would get sent roses sent each month. Affected by this, Maria becomes cold and indifferent. Later, when she taunts Oswald to send her a man to sleep with, he dies of a heart attack. As the announcement of the re-armament of West Germany is announced by Konrad Adeuner, the German Chancellor of the time, Hermann comes back a man. The German man. The director of the movie, Rainer Werner Fassbinder, remains steadfastly empathetic to the men and women of his country even after the unforgivable acts of genocide committed by them. Through the lives of German women, he has vividly captured the state of the country after the war. Bill doesn’t hesitate to come to Maria’s rescue, when Hermann strikes her. Even after getting beaten by her husband, she still sides with him, when he gets struck down by Bill. She categorically chooses her husband over Bill, despite all that he lacks, arguing that it is love. Oswald for all his wealth and power just doesn’t want to let go off a love that has only harmed him. Maria exploits his emotions for her own good but still he sticks to her. Maria’s mother asks her to live her life fully, while Maria stubbornly holds out for Hermann’s return. When he does come back from Canada, Maria promises him that everything that she has is his, even her heart. Through the power balance and manipulations in the relationships of several men with a German woman, the movie explores the rise of Germany as an economic power. Even when Maria is excited for her new life with Hermann, he tells her, “And I can love as a husband and not as someone you have to give his life back to.” The only person Maria wanted desperately in her life, the person whom she thinks would make her feel complete, does not appreciate the extent of her efforts and even deceives her. When Maria sees her country through to achieve the economic miracle, placing her mad love and need for family on a broken man (a broken nation), their personal life is exactly what falls apart spectacularly in the end. But still Germany the country found its miracle and won.
